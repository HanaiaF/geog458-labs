### Study area and dates:

The region I chose to explore with the AIS data is Tacoma, Washington. I was curious to see what marine vessel paths looked like around the holidays because I know the port of Tacoma has a lot of cargo ship traffic. This said, the time period is from 12/14, 2025 to 12/31 2025 and PST to answer these questions. In order to best optimize this research without having a dataset too large, I centered it around the port of Tacoma and avoided other areas such as University Place, etc.

### Interpretation: 

In the area I chose Tacoma, it seems that certain vessel types tend to stay in the same areas. For example, I noticed that when you select the passenger vessel filter, there is one prominent path from  the Ruston area to Vashon Island due to the ferry over there. Addtionally, the cargo vessel filters are concentrated around the Port of Tacoma. The traffic for both seem to be consistent through out the month, and the cargo vessels seem to see more action from 0:00 - 12:00 and later in the night from 20:00 - 23:00 from what I could see. The time we see vessel movement for passenger vessel from Ruston to Vashon is consistent with the hours listed on the website 5 am - 11 pm.

WSDOT website: https://wsdot.com/ferries/schedule/scheduledetailbyroute.aspx?route=pd-tal

### Feature proposal for a potential user: 

If we wanted to see how cargo vessel traffic varied throughout a specific timeframe, it would be helpful to see where that data would be concentrated within said timeframe. I believe this playback resource from Kepler would help with this. By having the bars in the timeline to show data concentrtions, we could see when to scrub the playback to get the most insight.
